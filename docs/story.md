# The Unsung: story outline

Working draft, Nov 2026. Original setting only (see `CLAUDE.md`). This is a direction to build from, not a final script.

## The heart of it

A man who has lost his identity, who happens to be a terrifying fighter, going through a war.

He has saved the realm, and will save it again, and nobody knows. If he does the job well, nothing happens, and nothing happening looks like nothing at all. People only notice failure. The only one who truly knows him is the enemy he locked away.

Themes to keep:
- Doing the work without being thanked.
- What you leave behind in the people you train.
- Losing someone right after they come into their own.
- Choosing peace after war.

## The hero

- **Unnamed.** Not erased or struck out: nobody ever knew his name, because the years in which anyone did are behind the Door. When the muster clerk asks, he says "Write whatever you like." From then on he is called by his rank (Captain, later). Nobody in the story ever says his name, except, perhaps, the Usurper.
- Quiet, awkward among ordinary people, at home only in a fight. He doesn't know how to make small talk, and he doesn't tell stories about himself because his only true one sounds like madness.
- He grows over the game, mostly because Corin won't leave him alone.

## The Usurper and the Glass Door

- Before the game begins, the Usurper tried to take the realm, and the hero stopped him in a vault under the desert ruins, sealing him behind the Glass Door.
- **He can't be killed, only shut out of time.** The Door doesn't hold a body; it holds the years he took. Sealing him puts those years behind the glass with him.
- **So the world doesn't forget it: it never lived it.** The hero walks out into the realm as it was before the Usurper rose. There was no usurpation for anyone to remember.
- **The hero did not know the cost.** He closed the Door to end it. Nobody sent him back, and he isn't returned to his own past: he is left in a world that has none for him. Whoever he was in those years, and whoever knew him, went behind the glass too. That is why nobody knows his name.
- He remembers because he closed the Door. The Usurper remembers because he is inside it.
- When the Door cracks (Act II), those years leak back out. That is how Vessa comes to know things only the Usurper could, and why the raids find the hero's road and waystation.
- None of this is said to the player in the prologue. It is shown: a blow that should kill him doesn't take, and the sealing runs the vault backwards.
- The Usurper is the only one who remembers. That is his weapon: he taunts the hero with it. "You and I are the only ones who know what you did." His enemy is his only witness.
- Through the war, the Usurper's will leaks out through the Witches of the dunes. The hero is the only one who understands what is really coordinating the desert, and he can't say so.
- Art: the Door design already in the game (leaded panes, chained gold seal, glass shards, ward ring, the vault dressing) is this place.

## Corin

- A squire assigned to him early in Act I. Outgoing, curious, stubborn. He isn't trying to become the captain; he challenges him, asks the questions the captain won't answer, and drags him into the life of the camp.
- The mentorship runs both ways. The captain teaches Corin to fight; Corin teaches the captain to be a person again. By the end they are closer to brothers than master and student.
- He dies in Act II, right after he comes into his own (see the Long Cut).

## The border war

The desert frontier heats up mission by mission, which gives the campaign its escalation (think *Dune*: sand, scarcity, an enemy that knows the land):
1. Scattered raids, easily driven off.
2. Raids that are clearly coordinated: feints, flanks, timed waves.
3. Raids aimed at things that only matter to someone who knows the captain: the road he walked out on, the waystation where he worked. This is where the Usurper's hand shows.

Vessa, the Witches' Hand, is the face of it: a recurring enemy commander who speaks the Usurper's words.

## Prologue: the climax nobody saw

0. **The halls (opening, playable; added Nov 2026).** He comes down through the halls above the vault, past the dead of both sides: the many who tried. One knight is still alive and tells him what they learned: the Usurper bleeds and does not die, and the Door below is what he came for; put him through it and shut it. He cuts his way down through the Usurper's guard. (Her lines are a draft. The fallen knights wear his colours.)
1. **The Glass Door (playable).** The last moments of the fight in the vault. The Usurper is beaten to his knees. The hero cuts him down, takes it for the end, and walks away; a few steps on the vault shakes, and behind him the Usurper gets up again. The hero puts him behind the glass, and the vault runs backwards around him. Parting words: "Only you will remember this."
1b. **The walk back up (playable; added Nov 2026).** He climbs back through the halls. The dead, the glass and the blood are gone, and daylight comes down the stair. Nothing is said.
2. **The road.** He walks out into a world where nothing happened. No crowds, no thanks. A caravan passes him and no one looks twice.
3. **Drifting (cards or short scenes).** Years of labouring jobs. He can't settle and doesn't fit.
4. **The waystation.** Desert raiders hit the waystation where he works. He takes up a fallen guard's sword. The first real fight of the game is him, a nobody in their eyes, cutting through them. A guard officer arrives at the end and sees what he did. (Also the first sign of the border war.)
5. **The muster.** The officer brings him in. The trial bout is the tutorial. The clerk asks his name: "Write whatever you like." He is sent to the worst posting on the frontier.

## Act I: The Western Watch

- He serves on the frontier with the Third Company and rises to captain because he is the one who keeps people alive.
- Corin is assigned to him, a reluctant pairing at first.
- Missions: patrols and skirmishes, then the raids start getting organised.
- **The first night at the ruins (the current playable slice)** belongs here: the spar, the raid, holding the Narrows while the wounded reach the sanctum, Vessa. Everyone lives. It is the version of the Narrows where the choice costs nothing.
- Quiet scenes in camp between missions: short, a little awkward, mostly Corin pulling him into ordinary life.

### Midpoint: stranded

- The captain and Corin are cut off from the company deep in the desert for several days.
- Around the fire, Corin talks about his family and what he'll do after the war. The captain nearly tells him about the Door, and can't.
- They find proof the raids are directed: glass shards worked into a raid leader's charm, Vessa speaking things only the Usurper could know.

## Act II: the war turns

- Raids now target what matters to the captain personally. Vessa taunts him with the Usurper's knowledge.
- He realises the Door is weakening, and he can't tell anyone why.
- Corin comes into his own: trusted with his own men, starting to surpass his teacher in ways the captain didn't expect.

### The Long Cut

- The company escorts civilians and supplies through a narrow canyon pass. It's an ambush: rockfalls cut the column apart.
- Soldiers are trapped behind the enemy line. Corin goes for them and uses the Still Draw the captain taught him. It works, and he saves them.
- The captain sees his teaching live in someone else. It is everything he hoped for.
- The rescue exposes Corin to Vessa, and she kills him before the captain can reach him.
- The captain breaks the encirclement and gets the survivors out. The line holds.
- Corin dies knowing he succeeded. The narrow pass mirrors the Narrows from the first night: the same choice, this time at full price.

## Act III: the second time

- The survivors are celebrated as heroes. The captain is offered honours and a command, and declines them.
- He goes alone to the Glass Door, which is cracking.
- Final fights: Vessa, then the Usurper at the Door.
- The Usurper's last taunt: now there is no one left who knows him, only the Usurper.
- He seals the Door anyway and walks out again. Nobody knows, again. He has saved the realm twice.

## Epilogue

- He leaves the guard and settles somewhere quiet, choosing peace.
- Last image: years later, at a muster yard, a young recruit practises the Still Draw, taught by one of the soldiers Corin saved in the Long Cut. The teaching survived, passed on without anyone's name, which is the only legacy the captain was ever going to have.

## Open questions

- Settled and built: Vessa survives the first night. She is driven off and withdraws at dawn.
- Built so far: the cold open at the Door, the waystation fight and the muster's trial bout are playable; the road and the drifting years are cards. As built (first draft, for the owner to correct): the muster is held in the waystation's own yard the next morning, not somewhere the officer brings him to; he wears a desert head-wrap in these years, so his face stays hidden; the officer, sergeant, clerk and drover are unnamed; all of their lines are drafts. The waystation teaches the roll and the heavy blow, and the bout is fought against a shield; parry, bash and side roll are still taught in Corin's spar, which has not been moved to the muster.
- What the Usurper looks like. The game uses a placeholder (black iron, glass-teal trim).
- The captain's eye wound mid-Vessa fight: keep only if it means something in this story (it could come from the Long Cut instead). Undecided.
- Does the Usurper ever say the hero's name, and does the player ever hear it?
- What does the Usurper want, and where did he come from? Not yet written. (Why he is sealed and not killed is settled: see The Usurper and the Glass Door.)
- When does the captain understand what closing the Door cost? He doesn't know in the prologue.
- How many missions per act, and which ones introduce new enemy types (more shield-bearers, allied soldiers who fight).
- The quiet camp scenes: how much is playable and how much is dialogue.
