# The Unsung: story outline

Working draft, Nov 2026. Original setting only (see `CLAUDE.md`). This is a direction to build from, not a final script.

## The heart of it

A man who has lost his identity, who happens to be a terrifying fighter, going through a war.

He has saved the realm, and will save it again, and nobody knows. If he does the job well, nothing happens, and nothing happening looks like nothing at all. People only notice failure. The only one who truly knows him is the enemy he locked away.

Themes to keep:
- Doing the work without being thanked.
- What you leave behind in the people you train.
- Losing someone right after they come into their own.
- Choosing peace after war.

Tone (owner, Nov 2026): brutal, in the manner of the *Red Rising* books. Characters the player has already met and come to like die, but only after they have become friends. See The cost.

## The hero

- **Unnamed.** Not erased or struck out: nobody ever knew his name, because the years in which anyone did are behind the Door. When the muster clerk asks, he says "Write whatever you like." From then on he is called by his rank (Captain, later). Nobody in the story ever says his name, except, perhaps, the Usurper.
- Quiet, awkward among ordinary people, but not mute: he answers the people beside him, in few words (owner, 16 Nov 2026). On the frontier he goes by his old job, "Yard hand", until he is made captain; at home only in a fight. He doesn't know how to make small talk, and he doesn't tell stories about himself because his only true one sounds like madness.
- He grows over the game, mostly because Corin won't leave him alone.

## The Usurper and the Glass Door

- Before the game begins, the Usurper tried to take the realm, and the hero stopped him in a vault under a court far from the frontier, sealing him behind the Glass Door.
- **The Door is his.** It is an instrument that holds years. The dying knight in the halls says so ("It is his. It is what he came down here for."). He came down to it to make his reign permanent: to hold the years he had taken so they could never be taken back. The hero turned it on him. (Owner liked this, Nov 2026.)
- **He can't be killed, only shut out of time.** The Door doesn't hold a body; it holds the years he took. Sealing him puts those years behind the glass with him.
- **So the world doesn't forget it: it never lived it.** The hero walks out into the realm as it was before the Usurper rose. There was no usurpation for anyone to remember.
- **Who exists afterwards (settled, Nov 2026).** Everyone who stood against him went behind the glass with him, whole: they do not exist in the world the hero walks out into. The knight who dies in the halls is not alive somewhere as a stranger; she is gone. The hero is the only one who stood against the Usurper and is still outside. That is why nobody knows his name: everyone who did is behind the Door.
- **The hero did not know the cost when he chose.** He closed the Door to end it. Nobody sent him back, and he isn't returned to his own past: he is left in a world that has none for him. As built, the Usurper tells him as the Door shuts ("Everything I took goes in with me. My war. My years. Everyone who stood against me in them. Out there, none of it will have happened. Only you will remember this."), and the court above is shown running back from the war to a clear day. So he learns the cost at the moment he pays it.
- He remembers because he closed the Door. The Usurper remembers because he is inside it.
- **Distance.** The Door is a long way from the waystation and from the frontier, and stays far off through the campaign. He can't go and look at it, which is why he can come to doubt it ever happened. The ruins of the first night are a different place. Act III includes the long road back.
- **The crack.** It begins at the end of the drifting years, when the caravans start coming in late; the waystation raid is already the Usurper's reach. That is why Vessa, on the first night, already says "You hold a door for people who will never learn your name." In Act II the captain realises the Door is weakening.
- The Usurper is the only one who remembers. That is his weapon: he taunts the hero with it. "You and I are the only ones who know what you did." His enemy is his only witness.
- Through the war, the Usurper's will leaks out through the Witches of the dunes. The hero is the only one who understands what is really coordinating the desert, and he can't say so.
- Glass is his mark, as built: the glass crown and sword, the glass growing out of him, the glass guards, the glass thickening through the halls toward the Door.
- Art: the Door design already in the game (leaded panes, chained gold seal, glass shards, ward ring, the vault dressing) is this place.

## Corin

- **First met at the beacon (decided, Nov 2026).** He is the wounded corporal's kid brother, who followed him into the Watch too young. The corporal keeps him as a runner. At the beacon he is a scared boy under the rock with the patrol.
- The corporal dies of his wound after the rescue. Corin has nobody, and is given to the man who held the rock, as his squire. A reluctant pairing on both sides: Corin has just lost the only family he had in the Watch and doesn't want another master; the captain doesn't want anyone.
- The player grows up with him. By the first night at the ruins he is the outgoing, curious, stubborn squire already built: he challenges the captain, asks the questions he won't answer, and drags him into the life of the camp. He isn't trying to become the captain.
- The mentorship runs both ways. The captain teaches Corin to fight; Corin teaches the captain to be a person again. By the end they are closer to brothers than master and student, which is what Corin lost at the beacon.
- He dies in Act II, right after he comes into his own (see the Long Cut).

## The cost

The war keeps taking the people who know him. The Door took everyone who knew him in the first life; the war takes the second. This is the Usurper's strategy, not chance: strip away everyone who knows the captain until the Usurper is the only one left, which is exactly what his last taunt claims. So each death takes a witness, not just a soldier. Deaths are scripted story beats; in play, allies who are beaten down still go to one knee and get up after the fight.

A first spacing, to be argued with:
- **The corporal** (Corin's brother): dies of the beacon wound, early. The cost that puts Corin with the captain.
- **Brann:** on the patrol he says "I'm sticking close to him." He dies close to him, in Act II, on one of the raids aimed at the captain personally.
- **Hale:** the one who always gives the captain the credit ("That was him, Corporal"). Killed in the run-up to the Long Cut, so the losses are already stacking when Corin goes.
- **Corin:** the Long Cut.
- **Wren** survives. She is one of the soldiers Corin saves in the Long Cut, and she is the teacher in the epilogue. The captain held a rock so she could climb; Corin held a pass so she could live. Someone has to survive to carry the lesson.

## What he teaches

The Still Draw came over from the fan-fiction draft (owner, Nov 2026: change or drop what's needed). Direction: keep the beat, the captain teaches something and it outlives him, but make the thing taught this game's own. He has already held a narrow place alone so that others could get through, three times: the waystation gate, the beacon rock, the Narrows. That is the lesson. Corin holds the Long Cut the way the captain taught him, and Wren teaches it in the epilogue. His line in the spar still fits: "The draw is the easy part. Surviving the moment after is the skill." Its name is open; "the Still Draw" stays in the built text until it is replaced. There should be a scene where he finally teaches it to Corin, and it should cost him, because the lesson is what puts Corin within Vessa's reach.

## The border war

The desert frontier heats up mission by mission, which gives the campaign its escalation (think *Dune*: sand, scarcity, an enemy that knows the land):
1. Scattered raids, easily driven off.
2. Raids that are clearly coordinated: feints, flanks, timed waves.
3. Raids aimed at things that only matter to someone who knows the captain: the road he walked out on, the waystation where he worked, and his friends. This is where the Usurper's hand shows.

Vessa, the Witches' Hand, is the face of it: a recurring enemy commander who speaks the Usurper's words.

### Midpoint: stranded

- The captain and Corin are cut off from the company deep in the desert for several days.
- Around the fire, Corin talks about his brother and what he'll do after the war. The captain nearly tells him about the Door, and can't.
- They find proof the raids are directed: glass shards worked into a raid leader's charm, Vessa speaking things only the Usurper could know.

## Act II: the war turns

- Raids now target what matters to the captain personally, including his friends (Brann). Vessa taunts him with the Usurper's knowledge.
- He realises the Door is weakening, and he can't tell anyone why.
- Corin comes into his own: trusted with his own men, starting to surpass his teacher in ways the captain didn't expect.
- The captain finally teaches him the lesson he refused on the first night (see What he teaches). Hale dies in the run-up to the Long Cut.

### The Long Cut

- The company escorts civilians and supplies through a narrow canyon pass. It's an ambush: rockfalls cut the column apart.
- Soldiers are trapped behind the enemy line, Wren among them. Corin goes for them and holds the way open as the captain taught him. It works, and he saves them.
- The captain sees his teaching live in someone else. It is everything he hoped for.
- The rescue exposes Corin to Vessa, and she kills him before the captain can reach him.
- The captain breaks the encirclement and gets the survivors out. The line holds.
- Corin dies knowing he succeeded. The narrow pass mirrors the Narrows from the first night: the same choice, this time at full price.

## Act III: the second time

- The survivors are celebrated as heroes. The captain is offered honours and a command, and declines them.
- He goes alone, the long way back, to the Glass Door, which is cracking.
- Final fights: Vessa, then the Usurper at the Door.
- The Usurper's last taunt: now there is no one left who knows him, only the Usurper.
- He seals the Door anyway and walks out again. Nobody knows, again. He has saved the realm twice.

## Epilogue

- He leaves the guard and settles somewhere quiet, choosing peace.
- Last image: years later, at a muster yard, a young recruit practises what the captain taught, taught by Wren, whom Corin saved in the Long Cut. The teaching survived, passed on without anyone's name, which is the only legacy the captain was ever going to have.

## Slow cooking (owner likes these; not decided)

- **Why the desert, why glass.** Glass is sand made solid by fire, and sand measures time: the Door is an hourglass. When it cracks, time leaks out as sand: the desert creeps, the caravans run late, raiders rise out of the dunes, glass grows out of the forecourt. And the desert wipes out footprints, which is his whole life.
- **Seeing through it.** Glass is a seal you can see through and can't pass. The Usurper has watched the hero's life from behind it, which is how he knows the road and the waystation. Looking into the Door, the hero sees his own face over his enemy's.
- **His colours.** The order whose colours the fallen knights wear (the captain's red) was born in the taken years, so in the world afterwards the badge means nothing to anyone.
- **A sliver of the Door.** A shard lodged in his face at the sealing: his only proof the war happened, a reason to keep his face covered, and it lights as the Door cracks. (Replaces the eye wound, which is dropped.)
- **The offer at the Door.** Open it, and everyone who stood with him comes back: the knight, his people, his name. The war comes back with them. It comes right after he has lost Corin, when it is most tempting. He refuses, this time knowing the price. The Usurper speaks his name to tempt him; he answers "Captain will do", the name the living gave him.
- **The next keeper.** Hourglasses run out. The recruit in the epilogue is whoever turns it next time.
